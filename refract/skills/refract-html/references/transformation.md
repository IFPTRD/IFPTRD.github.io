# Transform meaning into usable content

This is an editorial example with fictional facts. Do not copy its facts, headings or structure into a user's guide. Its purpose is to show how detailed information can survive a substantial rewrite.

## Source passage

Facilities teams send routine maintenance requests by email. Every email needs to identify the project code and site, describe the problem and include a cost estimate; missing information must be supplied before the review period starts. Routine submissions close at 17:00 on 27 November 2026. Requests costing no more than $25,000 go to the local manager, while those above $25,000 require regional approval. The programme cannot fund a request exceeding $250,000. Once a complete request is received, review takes up to 10 business days. Applicants need to allow for that review period rather than treating submission as approval. Urgent safety faults are handled through the emergency maintenance channel immediately rather than waiting for the routine deadline. The emergency channel uses the same required information when it is available, but missing estimates must not delay reporting an urgent safety fault.

## Useful transformation

The material can become one specific page with four sections. There is no need for a hero banner, intervention advice or a screenshot of the source page.

**Submit a routine maintenance request** — a short paragraph states: "Email the project code, site, problem description and cost estimate by 17:00 on 27 November 2026."

**Approval and funding limits** — a table makes the different thresholds comparable:

| Estimated cost | Approval / eligibility |
| --- | --- |
| Up to and including $25,000 | Local manager |
| Above $25,000, up to and including $250,000 | Regional approval |
| Above $250,000 | Not eligible for programme funding |

**Review period** — "Allow up to 10 business days after the complete request is received. Supply missing information before that period can start. Submission does not constitute approval."

**Urgent safety faults** — "Report immediately through the emergency maintenance channel; do not wait for the routine deadline. Provide the same information where available, but do not delay reporting because an estimate is missing."

This changes wording, groups related rules, makes boundaries explicit and preserves every substantive distinction. Exact values and their meaning remain. The output is shorter because the expression is clearer, not because an issue, condition or exception was removed. A different passage may need more prose rather than a table.

## Fidelity is not transcription

For ordinary explanation, verify equivalent meaning after rewriting. Reserve `required_text` for precision-sensitive fragments such as "$25,000", "$250,000", "17:00 on 27 November 2026" and "10 business days", plus genuinely verbatim passages if present. Verify their associated comparison operators, conditions and scope in the independent semantic review; matching a number alone does not prove the rule is correct. Do not add redundant copies of original sentences merely to satisfy an exact-match check.

When facts are distributed across multiple source sections, integrate them into a coherent topic and privately map the source units to those blocks. Keep meaningful agenda/procedure order where needed, but do not inherit PDF pagination.

## Visuals are selective

A paragraph and ordinary table should become editable text and a table. A source map or seating diagram may remain an image, cropped to that visual and its necessary legend/caption. Do not embed the surrounding PDF page, duplicate all extracted prose above its screenshot, or turn every original page into an image. If extraction fails, disclose that gap rather than representing images as successful conversion.

## Final editorial questions

- Can the reader tell where to find a specific answer from the navigation and headings?
- Is the answer complete, including conditions, limits and exceptions?
- Has the writing been made clearer rather than merely copied or chopped into arbitrary chunks?
- Does each table/list/visual improve comprehension for this particular material?
- Are supporting details integrated without repeating the same content in several forms?
- Would this actually reduce the reader's work compared with opening the source?

These are judgment checks. No count of pages, words, cards or images can establish editorial quality.
